# Chapter II Writing Rules

The researcher's standing rules for Chapter II (Review of Related Literature), saved 2026-11-22. They add to [[Writing Rules]]. Where the two differ, this note wins for Chapter II: source years are 2015–2026, at least 30 sources, at least 10 Word pages.

## Part C. Rules for all sections

1. **Opening paragraph** under every heading and subheading: a separate 2–3 sentence paragraph right after the heading. Its first sentence begins with the exact words "This section" and says what the section covers.
2. **Basic academic English:** familiar, precise words; one main idea per sentence; explain technical terms the first time. Each paragraph has one clear topic and connects smoothly to the next (fluidity and continuity).
3. **Source quality:** as recent as possible, objective and unbiased, relevant to DiviRush, and based on real original data.
4. **Publication years:** only sources published 2015–2026, checked from the publisher, journal or original document (an access date or website copyright year does not count). Prefer the most recent. For an important theory older than 2015, cite a 2015–2026 source that explains or applies it, and still credit the original author. List any pre-2015 or undated source in [[Open Questions]] for replacement.
5. **Length:** at least 10 Word pages in the [[D-005 Manuscript Format]], not counting the bibliography (roughly 3,000–4,000 words). Reach it with real discussion, not repetition or padding. Confirm the page count in Word.
6. **Citations:** at least 30 different sources cited in the text, counted as unique bibliography entries. Every one real, read, from 2015–2026, cited in the text and listed in the bibliography.
7. **Report each study honestly:** purpose, method, participants (number, grade or age, country), main findings and limitations. Never make a finding sound stronger than the source says.
8. **Other studies do not prove DiviRush works.** Write "this supports examining…" or "this informs the design of…", never "this proves DiviRush will improve…".
9. Keep a product's **described features** separate from **evidence** that it improves learning.
10. No "no similar app exists" or "this is the first" without a source. Describe a gap only as a limit of the reviewed studies.
11. Every in-text citation has a matching bibliography entry, and every bibliography entry is cited in the text.
12. **Never invent** a source, author, year, title, finding, statistic, quote, URL or DOI. Cite only sources actually opened and read, or supplied by the researcher. A source that cannot be opened or verified is not used.

## Part D. Required structure and section rules

Headings, in order:

- CHAPTER II
- REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE (short chapter introduction)
- Related Literature / Theoretical Background
- Related Studies
  - Related Projects
  - Synopsis
- BIBLIOGRAPHY

Related Projects and Synopsis are subsections, to be confirmed with the adviser.

**Source spread** (total 30 or more): Theoretical Background about 8–10; Related Studies about 14–16 (3–4 per specific objective); Related Projects about 4–6 (official pages or store listings, and studies about them).

### Related Literature / Theoretical Background

- Outline first, then write.
- Start with **one anchor theory** that explains how learners learn from this kind of game activity. If none is chosen, recommend one from a real source, explain in 2–3 sentences why it fits DiviRush, and add "Confirm anchor theory with adviser" to [[Open Questions]].
- Explain the theory in plain words, apply it to DiviRush's actual activities, and state where it does not fully fit.
- Add 2–3 **supporting theories** or bodies of literature (for example game-based learning, mobile learning, practice and feedback in Mathematics, motivation), each connected back to the anchor theory.
- End with a short paragraph on how the theories work together and how the next section follows.

### Related Studies

- Organize the studies in the order of the Chapter I specific objectives. For each objective: a short paragraph on what the objective needs, 3–4 related studies, then a paragraph on what this means for DiviRush.
- Prefer elementary Mathematics (especially division or basic operations), digital game-based learning, mobile learning, and Philippine or Southeast Asian classrooms. Include real Philippine studies where they exist.
- For each study: author and year, purpose, design and method, participants and setting, main findings, limitations, and how it differs from DiviRush's grade, topic or setting.
- Include mixed or negative findings when they exist.

### Related Projects

- 3–6 real apps or games similar to DiviRush, each with an official page or store listing that was opened.
- For each: name, developer, platform, target age or grade, topic, main activities, offline or online, cost if stated, and how DiviRush differs.
- A comparative matrix: Project | Developer | Platform | Target users | Topic | Main activities | Offline? | Difference from DiviRush.
- Suggest where screenshots could go, with the source to credit. Never fabricate screenshots.
- State that product descriptions are not evidence of learning effectiveness.

### Synopsis

3–5 paragraphs: what the theories and studies suggest for DiviRush's design, what the reviewed evidence does not show, the gap DiviRush addresses (as a limit of the reviewed studies), and what still needs testing (content accuracy, curriculum fit, device compatibility, learning outcomes).

### Bibliography

- Opening sentence: "This section lists the sources cited in Chapter II."
- Only cited sources (at least 30), APA 7th edition, alphabetical, with DOI or URL.

## Amendment 2026-11-22

The researcher changed two rules after reviewing the first drafts. These replace rule 5 (length) and limit how rule 7 is written:

- **Citation length:** each cited source is discussed in at most 1–2 sentences. Pack purpose, participants and main finding into the first sentence and the key limitation into the second. Explanation and application paragraphs without a citation may be longer.
- **Length:** the chapter body (not counting the bibliography) is 10–15 Word pages in the [[D-005 Manuscript Format]].
- **Related Projects:** three projects only.
- **Sections:** follow [[Manuscript Outline - BSIT (Table of Contents)]] exactly: CHAPTER II, REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE, Related Literature / Theoretical Background, Related Studies, BIBLIOGRAPHY. No subsection headings. Theories, objectives, related projects and the summary run as paragraphs inside the two sections, linked by transition sentences. This replaces the heading list in Part D.

## Saving rules for Chapter II

- Never delete or overwrite an existing note; save a new version with the date in its name. [[Open Questions]] is added to, not replaced.
- Keep the researcher's original files (Word, PDF, survey sheets) unchanged.
